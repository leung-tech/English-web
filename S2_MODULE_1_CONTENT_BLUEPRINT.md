# S2 Module 1 Content Blueprint: Experiences and Choices

**Author:** Manus AI  
**Status:** Planned next implementation  
**Audience:** Hong Kong S2 learners who have completed the S1 Bridge and S1 Core Path in Primary English Studio.  
**Boundary:** All texts, questions and models will be original bilingual practice. They are not official Education Bureau or HKEAA examination materials.

## 1. Purpose and learner transition

The first S2 module should convert S1 sentence-level accuracy into connected decisions, comparisons and explanations. It builds from the completed S1 sequence of past events, future plans, comparison, question forms, school-life vocabulary, evidence finding and purposeful response. This staged four-skills approach is consistent with the Education Bureau’s public English Language Education curriculum documentation and its junior-secondary reading-and-writing-across-the-curriculum resource direction.[1] [2]

> **Module question:** How can students explain an experience, compare realistic choices and support a recommendation with a reason and an example?

## 2. Module identity and release scope

| Field | Specification |
|---|---|
| **Module title** | S2 Develop: Experiences and Choices |
| **Chinese title** | 中二發展：經驗與選擇 |
| **Primary language focus** | Present perfect versus simple past; `for` and `since`; `already` and `yet`; modals; comparatives and superlatives. |
| **Contexts** | School service, healthy habits, travel choices, community events and responsible technology use. |
| **Student outputs** | Compare two choices; explain a past experience and its present relevance; make a recommendation; give a supported opinion. |
| **Learning assets** | 16 grammar/editing items, 18 vocabulary questions, 4 paired-text sets / 12 questions, 3 listening scripts / 12 questions, 3 writing tasks and 3 speaking tasks. |
| **Route placement** | Grammar and vocabulary in Apply; paired texts in Read; listening/speaking in Listen; response tasks in Write. |
| **User-facing label** | `S2 DEVELOP · 原創練習` on every module card and task introduction. |

## 3. First build: grammar and editing pack

The first implementation should be **S2 Grammar: Experiences and Choices**. It is the cleanest continuation from S1 because it introduces aspect and choice language through familiar, non-exam contexts before students meet longer paired texts.

| Item range | Target language | Context | Student decision | Feedback focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1–3 | Present perfect vs simple past | A completed school service project | Decide whether the time is finished or linked to now | `yesterday/last term` versus an unfinished or current connection. |
| 4–5 | `for` / `since` | Joining a club and building a reading habit | Select a duration or starting point | `for + period`; `since + starting time`. |
| 6–7 | `already` / `yet` | Preparing for a trip or submitting work | Place the adverb in a question or statement | Position and meaning in present perfect sentences. |
| 8–10 | Modals | Advice, rules and responsible choices | Distinguish obligation, advice and possibility | `must`, `should`, `might` plus base verb. |
| 11–13 | Comparatives / superlatives | Comparing two school or travel options | Choose an evidence-based comparison | One-syllable, multi-syllable and irregular forms. |
| 14–16 | Mixed editing and paragraph cohesion | A short recommendation post | Repair tense, modal or comparison errors | Evidence, linking and register must match the purpose. |

Every item should use one school, community or daily-life mini-text, four answer options, English-first wording, Traditional Chinese support, a context-specific explanation and a short revision hint. The final three items should each cite the relevant sentence within a short paragraph so that editing is not reduced to isolated grammar selection.

## 4. Vocabulary and word-choice pack

The first S2 vocabulary pack should use 18 items grouped around experiences, decisions and comparison. Each card becomes one interactive multiple-choice practice item with a definition, Traditional Chinese cue, original model sentence and four options.

| Theme | Target words | Word-choice habit |
|---|---|---|
| **Experience and progress** | achieve, adapt, challenge, contribute, experience, familiar | Relate a past action to learning or present development. |
| **Choices and recommendations** | compare, consider, option, recommend, suitable, value | Explain why one choice fits a specific need. |
| **Community and responsibility** | benefit, conserve, convenient, effective, impact, practical | Link a suggestion to a realistic community result. |

## 5. Paired-text reading workshop

The reading module should be called **S2 Reading: Compare and Connect**. It must teach students to move beyond retrieving a single detail by connecting purpose, audience, comparison and evidence across two short original texts. Each set should contain two clearly labelled texts, a total length appropriate for lower secondary independent reading, and three questions: one direct detail, one connection/comparison and one purpose, tone or evidence question.

| Text set | Text A | Text B | Core comparison | Question focus |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1. Reading challenge** | Student blog about a daily reading habit | Library notice about a reading challenge | Personal experience versus practical information | Detail, purpose, useful support. |
| **2. School transport** | Student email recommending a bus route | Short transport survey summary | Individual recommendation versus group evidence | Comparison, pronoun reference, evidence. |
| **3. Community service** | Volunteer reflection | Club recruitment poster | Reflection versus persuasion | Tone, audience, relevant action. |
| **4. Screen time choices** | Magazine-style opinion paragraph | Student poll results | Claim versus simple data | Vocabulary in context, claim/evidence, balanced conclusion. |

The interface should display the two texts side-by-side on a desktop and sequentially on a phone, while keeping a question’s evidence accessible. Each feedback explanation should identify the relevant text and either quote or precisely name the supporting phrase.

## 6. Listening, writing and speaking continuation

| Skill | First release contents | Interactive pattern |
|---|---|---|
| **Listening** | School-trip planning meeting, community-service interview and radio segment about healthy routines | Replayable speech-synthesis script; 12 questions on people, time, change, reason, comparison and recommendation; transcript only after marking. |
| **Writing** | Recommendation email, experience-and-learning recount, short comparison paragraph | Purpose/audience card, paragraph planner, 100–120 word self-check and no automated quality score. |
| **Speaking** | Recommend an option, compare two activities, respond politely to a different view | Replayable model; 45–60 second plan; self-check for opinion, reason, example and response. |

## 7. Acceptance and safeguarding requirements

The S2 release is ready only when every objective item has a stable unique ID, valid answer index, bilingual prompt/explanation, original-context statement and useful hint. Every text must state its genre and communication purpose. Every script requires a title, Traditional Chinese title, replay control and transcript-after-check flow. Writing and speaking results must be framed as **practice completion and self-reflection**, never as an automated assessment of language quality or student ability.

The responsive interface must preserve both source texts on a small screen without horizontal overflow. It must retain the platform’s local-only progress design. No student account, upload or external profile is required.

| Sprint | Deliverable | Dependency | Outcome |

| **3** | Paired-text renderer and four reading sets | S1 Core reading display and responsive layout | A 12-question compare-and-connect workshop. |

| **5** | Mixed checkpoint and public deployment verification | Complete S2 assets | Local feedback by skill tag and validated online practice. |
