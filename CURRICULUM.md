# Primary English Studio — Curriculum Blueprint

## Purpose

This curriculum supports Hong Kong primary pupils from **Primary 1 to Primary 6** through a balanced programme of vocabulary, grammar, reading, writing, listening and speaking. The learning route begins with phonics, everyday language and sentence confidence in the junior years, then progresses towards connected texts, organised writing and examination-style language use in the senior years.

> **Learning principle:** every activity should help pupils understand English, use it meaningfully and notice one clear next step for improvement.

## Grade-level scope

| Grade | Learning focus | Vocabulary themes | Grammar and sentence patterns | Reading and writing expectations | Listening and speaking expectations |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| P1 | Phonics, oral confidence and basic language chunks | Greetings, family, school items, animals, fruit, numbers, toys, colours and clothes | `am / is / are`, `a / an`, singular and plural nouns, personal pronouns, `my / your / his / her`, simple location words | Read labels and 50-word picture texts; complete words and simple sentences; write 1–3 linked sentences | Recognise letter sounds, follow classroom instructions, introduce oneself and read a short dialogue aloud |
| P2 | Everyday routines and accurate simple sentences | Body parts, food and drinks, home, places, weather, seasons and daily routines | `have / has`, simple present, third-person `-s`, `there is / are`, countable nouns, `can / cannot`, present continuous | Read notices, timetables and short descriptions; write 3–4 related sentences | Listen for daily details, answer simple questions and describe familiar pictures |
| P3 | Connected ideas and familiar past experiences | Jobs, transport, hobbies, shapes, size, health and feelings | Simple past, present continuous, comparisons, frequency adverbs, `and / but / because`, place and movement prepositions | Read 80–120 word stories; identify key details; write a short diary or picture story | Listen to a short story for people, places and actions; retell a sequence with support |
| P4 | Information organisation and richer language use | Festivals, cultures, healthy living, recycling, environment and community facilities | Future forms, present perfect introduction, `should / must`, quantity expressions, superlatives, sequence linkers | Read emails, diaries, instructions and factual texts; write organised paragraphs and practical messages | Listen for the main idea and supporting details; give a 1-minute prepared presentation |
| P5 | Tense comparison, inference and purposeful writing | Geography, travel, natural disasters, technology, famous people and biographies | Present perfect with `for / since`, tense contrast, passive voice, first conditional, relative clauses, connectives and infinitives | Read reports, posters, charts and news-style texts; write 80–120 word emails, narratives and suggestions | Take notes from longer conversations and broadcasts; discuss an opinion with reasons |
| P6 | Secondary-school readiness and independent expression | Secondary-school life, aspirations, careers, adolescent challenges, volunteering and current social topics | Past continuous with simple past, present perfect versus past simple, passive voice, second conditional, gerunds and infinitives, reported speech and complex linkers | Read multi-format and viewpoint texts; write 100–150 word emails, stories, articles and proposals | Infer speaker attitudes, take concise notes and deliver a structured 1–2 minute presentation |

## Four-skill practice model

The platform is organised as four visible skill routes. Vocabulary and grammar are integrated within the routes rather than presented as an isolated subject stream. This layout helps pupils and families see why language knowledge matters in authentic reading, writing and listening tasks.

| Route | What pupils practise | Sample task formats | Core feedback focus |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Read** | Understanding key information, vocabulary in context, inference and text organisation | Picture text, story, email, notice, report and chart questions | Locate evidence, identify the main idea and make a sensible inference |
| **Write** | Sentence accuracy, ideas, organisation and editing | Word ordering, sentence building, gap-fill, proofreading, email and story planning | Subject–verb agreement, tense control, punctuation and linking words |
| **Listen & Speak** | Listening for gist and detail, pronunciation and short spoken responses | Replayable audio prompt, multiple-choice listening item, shadow reading and oral prompt | Listen before choosing, notice key words and speak in complete sentences |
| **Language Lab** | Grade-level vocabulary, phonics, spelling, grammar and sentence patterns | Vocabulary choice, spelling, grammar usage and sentence transformation | Choose the form that matches the meaning, subject and time clue |

## Assessment habits

Pupils should be encouraged to circle question key words in reading tasks, locate time clues in grammar tasks, use planning words in writing and preview options before listening. Error feedback should be short, specific and encouraging. A local review list should allow pupils to practise missed questions again.

## Interface direction

The site will use a **bright study-studio layout** instead of a multi-subject workbook dashboard. A persistent grade selector, a four-skill learning grid, a compact learning progress panel and a clear practice screen will make the learning route easy to understand on desktop and mobile. The main instructional language remains Chinese for accessibility, while all target language, questions and models are in English.

The listening route uses the browser’s built-in speech function so that pupils can replay material at an appropriate pace without requiring an external audio account. Each listening item includes a visible replay button, an English transcript only after checking the answer, and a short speaking follow-up prompt.

## Content boundaries

All example sentences, passages and prompts are age-appropriate. P1–P3 tasks use short, concrete and familiar contexts. P4–P6 tasks gradually introduce informative, reflective and opinion-based contexts, without assuming secondary-school vocabulary or abstract knowledge beyond the stated scope.

## Practice session sizes

| Stage | Suggested session size | Text and response length |
|---|---:|---|
| P1–P3 | 5–6 items | 1–3 sentence writing; up to 100-word reading/listening text |
| P4–P6 | 6–8 items | 1 short paragraph or 80–150-word extended-writing guidance; longer information texts |

## Completion indicators

A completed task updates local progress. Incorrect items are stored on the same device for later review. The review area groups items by the four skill routes so pupils, parents and teachers can quickly see the next most useful practice area.
