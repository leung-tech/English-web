# P4–P6 Writing Support Library

## How to use this support

Each resource accompanies one high-score model. Pupils should first identify the **genre** and **purpose**, then correct one or two common errors, practise precise topic vocabulary and adapt a sentence pattern with their own details. The aim is to make better writing choices, not to copy complete sentences without understanding them.

> **Check one priority at a time: format, content, organisation, accuracy, then vocabulary.**

## P4: Informal email — *Join Our Green Day*

| Common mistake | Better correction | Why it improves the writing |
|---|---|---|
| `Dear Amy, I invite you join Green Day.` | `Dear Amy, I would love you to join our Green Day.` | Use a friendly invitation phrase and the pattern *invite someone to join*. |
| No subject line or closing | Add `Subject: Join Our Green Day` and `Love, Kelly` | An email should have a recognisable format. |
| `We collect bottles yesterday.` | `We will collect used bottles on Friday.` | Use future language for a planned event. |

**Vocabulary expansion exercise:** Replace *good*, *old bottles*, *bring* and *fun* with **useful**, **used plastic bottles**, **remember to bring** and **enjoyable**.

**Sentence patterns:**

1. `Our school is holding [event] on [date].`
2. `First, we will [activity]. Then, we will [activity].`
3. `Please remember to bring [item] because [reason].`

## P4: Picture story — *The Lost Wallet*

| Common mistake | Better correction | Why it improves the writing |
|---|---|---|
| `Ben find a wallet and give teacher.` | `Ben found a wallet and took it to the school office.` | Keep the story in the past tense and use clear action verbs. |
| `Then... Then... Then...` | Use `At first`, `However`, `A few minutes later`, `In the end` | Sequence linkers make the plot easier to follow. |
| `Ben was happy.` | `Ben felt proud because he had done the right thing.` | Add a precise feeling and reason. |

**Vocabulary expansion exercise:** Replace *find*, *look*, *happy* and *give back* with **discover**, **look inside**, **relieved/proud** and **return**.

**Sentence patterns:**

1. `While [character] was [verb-ing], he/she saw [problem].`
2. `At first, [character] wanted to [action]. However, [reason], so...`
3. `In the end, [character] felt [feeling] because [reason].`

## P5: Formal email — *Ideas for a Better School Canteen*

| Common mistake | Better correction | Why it improves the writing |
|---|---|---|
| `Hi Principal, our canteen is bad.` | `Dear Principal Chan, I am writing to suggest two ways to improve our school canteen.` | Use a respectful, formal tone. |
| `Students need healthy food. It is good.` | `A fruit-and-yoghurt cup would give pupils a quick, healthy snack.` | Explain a specific benefit rather than making a vague claim. |
| `If pupils use cups, less cups throw away.` | `If more pupils used reusable cups, fewer plastic cups would be thrown away.` | Check plural nouns and use a clear conditional result. |

**Vocabulary expansion exercise:** Replace *good food*, *a lot of rubbish*, *cheap* and *make better* with **healthy choices**, **less plastic waste**, **at a reasonable price** and **improve**.

**Sentence patterns:**

1. `I am writing to suggest [number] ways to improve [place/service].`
2. `First, [suggestion]. This would [benefit].`
3. `If [condition], [positive result].`

## P5: Narrative — *A Rainy-Day Helper*

| Common mistake | Better correction | Why it improves the writing |
|---|---|---|
| `It rained. Mia went home. A boy cried.` | `Heavy rain was falling when Mia left the library.` | Create a setting by combining background and action. |
| `Mia helped the boy. It was good.` | `Mia opened her umbrella over the papers and picked up the wet worksheets.` | Show the helpful actions instead of only stating them. |
| `The mother came. End.` | `Although Mia missed the first bus, she smiled because she had helped someone.` | End with a consequence and reflection. |

**Vocabulary expansion exercise:** Replace *rained a lot*, *walked fast*, *wet papers* and *kind* with **heavy rain was falling**, **hurried**, **soaked worksheets** and **thoughtful**.

**Sentence patterns:**

1. `[Background action] when [sudden action happened].`
2. `Without waiting, [character] [helpful action].`
3. `Although [small difficulty], [character] did not mind because [reflection].`

## P6: Proposal — *A Quiet Study Corner*

| Common mistake | Better correction | Why it improves the writing |
|---|---|---|
| `The school should make a quiet place.` | `I am writing to propose a quiet study corner in the library.` | State the proposal and location precisely. |
| `Put tables there. It will be good.` | `Two tables could be placed near the back windows so that pupils are not interrupted.` | Give a practical action and explain its purpose. |
| `It is cheap and good for students.` | `The project would not require expensive equipment, but it could benefit many pupils.` | Use a more formal and persuasive tone. |

**Vocabulary expansion exercise:** Replace *quiet place*, *make*, *good for* and *not expensive* with **calm study area**, **establish/create**, **benefit** and **would not require expensive equipment**.

**Sentence patterns:**

1. `I am writing to propose [project] because [need].`
2. `[Resource] could be [past participle] near [place] so that [benefit].`
3. `If the project is successful, it may [positive result].`

## P6: Magazine article — *Small Acts, Stronger Community*

| Common mistake | Better correction | Why it improves the writing |
|---|---|---|
| No title; starts with `Volunteer service is good.` | Use a title and engaging question: `Have you ever helped someone and felt happier afterwards?` | An article needs to catch the reader’s attention. |
| `Volunteers help people. It is important.` | `Volunteers can read to younger children or help elderly visitors use digital devices.` | Specific examples develop an argument. |
| `Everyone should volunteer. Bye.` | `Let us take one small action this term and build a kinder community.` | Finish with a strong call to action. |

**Vocabulary expansion exercise:** Replace *help people*, *old people*, *good*, and *do volunteer work* with **support others**, **elderly visitors**, **meaningful/valuable**, and **take part in volunteer service**.

**Sentence patterns:**

1. `Have you ever [question] and [feeling/result]?`
2. `For example, pupils can [action], [action] or [action].`
3. `Although some pupils [concern], even [small action] can [positive result].`
4. `Let us [call to action] so that [community benefit].`

## Universal proofreading routine

| Read-through | What to check | Typical correction |
|---|---|---|
| First read: format | Title, subject line, greeting, closing, paragraphs | Add a missing email subject or article title. |
| Second read: task points | Who, what, when, where, reason, suggestion or ending | Add a missing required detail. |
| Third read: grammar | Tense, singular/plural nouns, subject–verb agreement, prepositions | Change `pupils was` to `pupils were`. |
| Fourth read: punctuation | Capital letters, full stops, commas and question marks | Change `i think` to `I think.` |
| Fifth read: word choice | Repeated basic words such as *good*, *nice* or *thing* | Replace with a precise topic word. |
