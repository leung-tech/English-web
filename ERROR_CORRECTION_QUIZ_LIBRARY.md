# P4–P6 Interactive Error-Correction Quiz Library

## Quiz design

Each high-score writing model receives a **three-question error-correction mini-quiz**. The question type is multiple choice: pupils select the strongest corrected sentence after reading a common weak version. Immediate bilingual feedback explains the grammar or genre choice. A three-question round is short enough to complete after reading a model, while still revisiting the most important error patterns.

> **The correct answer is the clearest and most accurate sentence for the writing task, not simply the longest option.**

| Year and genre | Error focus | Sample correction skill |
|---|---|---|
| P4 informal email | Future plans, invitation patterns, email format | Use *will* for a planned event and *invite someone to join* |
| P4 picture story | Simple past, past continuous, feelings | Keep events in the past and use *was/were + -ing* for background actions |
| P5 formal email | Formal register, subject–verb agreement, conditionals | State suggestions politely and link conditions to results clearly |
| P5 narrative | Past continuous, action verbs, reflective ending | Show a setting and complete the story with a consequence |
| P6 proposal | Proposal purpose, modal passive, formal persuasion | Use *could be + past participle* and a precise purpose |
| P6 magazine article | Subject–verb agreement, base verbs after *can*, linkers | Use engaging but grammatically controlled persuasive writing |

## P4 informal email quiz

| Weak version | Corrected answer | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| `Our school hold a Green Day this Friday.` | `Our school will hold a Green Day this Friday.` | A planned future event needs *will + base verb*. |
| `I invite you join our Green Day.` | `I would love you to join our Green Day.` | Use *invite someone to join* or a friendly invitation phrase. |
| `Please bring a reusable bottle because help school.` | `Please bring a reusable bottle because it helps our school.` | A complete reason needs a subject and verb. |

## P4 picture-story quiz

| Weak version | Corrected answer | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| `Ben find a wallet beside the bus stop.` | `Ben found a wallet beside the bus stop.` | A finished story event uses simple past. |
| `Ben walked home when he see the wallet.` | `Ben was walking home when he saw the wallet.` | Use past continuous for background action and simple past for a sudden event. |
| `Ben felt pride because he did the right thing.` | `Ben felt proud because he did the right thing.` | *Proud* is the adjective that describes Ben’s feeling. |

## P5 formal-email quiz

| Weak version | Corrected answer | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| `Hi Principal, the canteen is bad.` | `Dear Principal Chan, I am writing to suggest two improvements to the school canteen.` | A formal email needs a respectful greeting and purpose. |
| `The canteen could sells a fruit cup.` | `The canteen could sell a fruit cup.` | A modal verb is followed by a base verb. |
| `If pupils bring reusable cups, fewer plastic cups will be thrown away.` | `If pupils bring reusable cups, fewer plastic cups will be thrown away.` | In a likely future condition, use present after *if* and *will* in the result. |

## P5 narrative quiz

| Weak version | Corrected answer | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| `Heavy rain fell when Mia leave the library.` | `Heavy rain was falling when Mia left the library.` | Combine background action with a past event. |
| `Mia open her umbrella and pick up the sheets.` | `Mia opened her umbrella and picked up the sheets.` | Keep completed actions in simple past. |
| `Although Mia missed the bus, but she smiled.` | `Although Mia missed the bus, she smiled.` | Do not use *but* after *although*. |

## P6 proposal quiz

| Weak version | Corrected answer | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| `I propose make a quiet study corner.` | `I am writing to propose a quiet study corner in the library.` | State the proposal precisely in a formal opening. |
| `Two tables could placed near the windows.` | `Two tables could be placed near the windows.` | Modal passive form: *could be + past participle*. |
| `If the project successful, it may encourage pupils.` | `If the project is successful, it may encourage pupils.` | A condition clause needs a complete verb phrase. |

## P6 magazine-article quiz

| Weak version | Corrected answer | Tip |
|---|---|---|
| `Volunteer service are important.` | `Volunteer service is important.` | *Volunteer service* is singular. |
| `Pupils can to read stories to children.` | `Pupils can read stories to children.` | Use a base verb after *can*. |
| `Although some pupils are busy, but one morning can be meaningful.` | `Although some pupils are busy, one morning can be meaningful.` | Use *although* without *but*. |

## Quiz feedback rules

A correct answer receives a concise encouragement plus the reason that made it accurate. An incorrect answer reveals the corrected sentence and a single focused tip. The learner may proceed through all three questions and receives a simple completion score, such as **2/3 corrections accurate**. The score is a practice indicator, not an official assessment mark.
