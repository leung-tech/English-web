# S1–S3 Secondary English Studio: Hong Kong Curriculum and Senior-secondary Bridge Audit

**Audit date:** 22 August 2026
**Scope:** Secondary English Studio S1–S3 original-practice materials, including reading, writing, listening, speaking, contextual vocabulary, grammar, dialogues, games and evidence-response tasks.
**Status:** **Broadly aligned as original junior-secondary practice with a clearly bounded S3 senior-secondary bridge.** This is **not** an official Education Bureau or HKEAA endorsement, prescribed year-by-year syllabus, HKDSE question bank, marking scheme or score predictor.

> The audit uses official curriculum and assessment references to guide capability progression. It does not claim that every school uses the same sequence, text types, assessment weightings or internal examination format.

## 1. Reference framework

The Education Bureau maintains English Language Education curriculum documents across primary and secondary levels.[1] The 2017 English Language Education Key Learning Area guide provides an overall framework from Primary 1 to Secondary 6.[2] The HKEAA identifies the English Language Curriculum and Assessment Guide for Secondary 4–6 as the official senior-secondary English Language reference.[3]

An EDB Language Learning Support Section practice example further illustrates a productive bridge from Key Stage 3 to Key Stage 4: contextual vocabulary and language features; source material; text types; audience, tone, style and register; cohesive devices; evidence-aware writing; self-evaluation; and progressively more challenging tasks.[4] These are therefore treated as **transferable preparation skills**, not as copied or simulated public-examination materials.

## 2. Capability progression now implemented

| Stage | Main learning emphasis | Evidence in the Secondary English Studio | Alignment judgement |
|---|---|---|---|
| **S1 START / CORE** | Familiar school contexts; essential grammar and vocabulary; short practical reading/listening; clear functional responses. | School-life grammar, reading and cloze, listening, vocabulary, writing and speaking modules. | Appropriate junior-secondary foundation. |
| **S1 EXTEND** | Community interaction; giving reasons; polite collaboration; practical messages; feedback and short structured writing. | Community-and-voice modules, grammar quests, 30-round Phrase Builder, dialogue/speaking work and writing scaffolds. | Appropriate application stage that develops language use beyond isolated grammar. |
| **S2 DEVELOP / CONNECT / ACTION** | Comparing choices; identifying audience/purpose; media and digital literacy; community problem-solving; supported proposals. | Experiences-and-choices, messages-and-media and community/environment packs across all four skills. | Appropriate middle junior-secondary development, with contextual vocabulary and purposeful communication. |
| **S2 CONSOLIDATE** | Source checking; comparison of perspectives; limited evidence; fair response; audience-aware register; revision. | Evidence-and-viewpoints modules, grammar bank, context games and Digital & wellbeing missions. | Strong junior-secondary consolidation and a suitable lead-in to more skills-based senior-secondary work. |
| **S3 READY** | Evidence limitations; cautious claims; formal response; coherent organisation; source evaluation; integrated listening-to-speaking work. | Formal response grammar bank, DSE-bridge writing models, source-review tasks, integrated assessment/planner and simulations. | Suitable **senior-secondary bridge only**. It should not be described as official DSE preparation material or a predicted examination format. |

## 3. Four-skill and language-use check

| Capability area | Audit finding | Improvement retained or added |
|---|---|---|
| **Reading** | All S1–S3 stages include contextual texts and question sets. S2–S3 increasingly require comparison, source checks and evidence limits. | Keep questions tied to stated textual evidence; avoid treating an inference as a fact. |
| **Writing** | Existing S1–S3 modules include practical, narrative, proposal and formal-response scaffolds. | New S2 Digital & wellbeing writing challenges add a short audience-aware class-chat response and an evidence-aware two-week proposal, with planning, language bank, model, draft and self-check. |
| **Listening and speaking** | S1–S3 include listening scripts, dialogues, speaking prompts and S3 integration tasks. | Continue to use performance rubrics and self-checks; do not claim automated scoring of writing or speech quality. |
| **Grammar and vocabulary** | Grammar is generally embedded in school, community, media, evidence and formal-response contexts. | New S2 tasks add `does not establish that`, `according to the attendance record`, `set a reasonable boundary` and `adjust the schedule`; each is practised through a decision, not a decontextualised word list. |
| **Audience, purpose and register** | S2 media and S3 formal-response materials already address audience and purpose. | The new S2 writing tasks explicitly require a calm class-chat reply and a proposal for a class mentor, so learners choose tone according to audience. |
| **Evidence and reflection** | S2–S3 materials already use sources, limitations, trials, feedback and review language. | New tasks require cautious claims, source/date checking, a stated limitation and a plan to monitor a trial. This reflects the skills-based bridging approach described in the EDB example.[4] |

## 4. Current optimisation decisions

The audit did **not** replace materials with copied textbook or public-examination content. Instead, it reinforced the existing progression through three actions. First, every stage now shows a visible **Capability pathway · 能力範圍** note in the web interface, distinguishing S1 foundations, S2 applied junior-secondary learning and the bounded S3 bridge. Second, new S2 task-chain vocabulary asks students to match the strength of a claim to its evidence, attribute a source, set a realistic boundary and revise a plan after feedback. Third, the new writing challenges remain scaffolded and browser-local: they invite drafting and reflection but make no automated quality or score claim.

## 5. Limits and maintenance rules

The platform should continue to observe these rules.

| Rule | Reason |
|---|---|
| Label all materials **Original practice · 原創練習**. | The HKEAA’s senior-secondary guide is the official reference; this platform is not an official paper.[3] |
| Describe S3 materials as **DSE bridge / 高中銜接**, never as a predicted paper, official question or official marking scheme. | Maintains an accurate boundary between skills preparation and public assessment. |
| Keep S1–S2 tasks contextual and scaffolded before introducing denser formal-response demands. | Supports a progressive KS3–KS4 transition rather than an abrupt imitation of senior-secondary assessment.[4] |
| Preserve all four skills and language knowledge across stages. | The EDB framework covers English Language Education across the school years, while the audited design uses a broad English learning model rather than a grammar-only bank.[1] [2] |
| Keep writing and speaking feedback descriptive and self-reflective. | A static browser site cannot validly certify language quality or award an examination score. |
