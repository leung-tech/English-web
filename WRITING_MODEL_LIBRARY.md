# P4–P6 Assessment Writing Model Library

## Use of these models

The following models are **high-quality practice examples**, not fixed answers to memorise. Pupils should notice how each writer answers every part of the task, develops details, organises ideas and checks language. The actual marking scheme may vary by school; the accompanying rubric is a practical self-check rather than an official school score sheet.

> **A strong composition is clear, relevant, organised and accurate. It does not need difficult words in every sentence.**

## Practical self-assessment rubric

| Criterion | What a strong answer does | Self-check prompt |
|---|---|---|
| Task fulfilment | Responds to the purpose, audience, format and every required point | Have I answered every part of the question? |
| Content development | Adds specific actions, reasons, feelings or useful examples | Have I explained my ideas instead of only listing them? |
| Organisation | Uses a clear opening, logical paragraphs, suitable linkers and an ending | Can a reader follow my ideas easily? |
| Language accuracy | Controls tense, subject–verb agreement, spelling, punctuation and sentence endings | Have I checked my verbs, capital letters and full stops? |
| Vocabulary and variety | Uses topic words and a mixture of simple and longer sentences appropriately | Have I used precise words and at least one useful linker? |

A pupil may give each criterion **1–4 stars** for a 20-star self-review. This score is for improvement tracking only.

## Primary 4 models

### Model 1 — Informal email: *Join Our Green Day*

**Task:** Write an email to invite a friend to your school’s Green Day. Include the date, activities and what your friend should bring.

> To: Amy  
> Subject: Join Our Green Day  
>
> Dear Amy,  
>
> Our school is holding a Green Day this Friday, 14 June. I would love you to join me. The activity will begin at 3:30 p.m. in the playground.  
>
> First, we will collect used paper and plastic bottles. Then, we will make useful things from the bottles, such as pencil holders. Our teachers will also teach us how to sort rubbish correctly.  
>
> Please bring one clean plastic bottle and a reusable water bottle. I think the day will be fun because we can help our school and learn something new together.  
>
> Hope to see you there!  
>
> Love,  
> Kelly

**Why it works:** The email uses the correct format, includes every requested detail, uses sequence words and gives a reason for the invitation.

### Model 2 — Picture story: *The Lost Wallet*

**Task:** Write a story about a pupil who finds and returns a lost wallet.

> Last Monday, Ben was walking home after school when he saw a brown wallet beside the bus stop. He picked it up and looked inside. There was some money, an identity card and a small photo of a girl.  
>
> At first, Ben wanted to wait near the bus stop. However, it was getting dark, so he decided to take the wallet to the school office. The secretary checked the name on the card and called Ms Lee, a teacher at the school.  
>
> A few minutes later, Ms Lee arrived. She was very relieved because the wallet belonged to her. She thanked Ben warmly and said that he had done the right thing. Ben felt proud as he walked home. He learned that a small honest action could make a big difference.

**Why it works:** The story has a clear beginning, problem, action and ending. Past-tense verbs, feeling words and the linker *However* make the sequence easy to follow.

## Primary 5 models

### Model 3 — Formal email: *Ideas for a Better School Canteen*

**Task:** Write an email to your principal suggesting two improvements to the school canteen. Give reasons for your ideas.

> To: Principal Chan  
> Subject: Ideas for a Better School Canteen  
>
> Dear Principal Chan,  
>
> I am writing to suggest two ways to improve our school canteen. Last week, my class carried out a short survey. Many pupils said that they wanted healthier choices and less plastic waste.  
>
> First, the canteen could sell a fruit-and-yoghurt cup at a reasonable price. It would give pupils a quick, healthy snack before after-school activities. Second, the school could offer a small discount to pupils who bring reusable cups. If more pupils used their own cups, fewer plastic cups would be thrown away.  
>
> I hope you will consider these ideas. They could make our school healthier and cleaner.  
>
> Yours sincerely,  
> Chris Wong

**Why it works:** The writer uses a polite formal register, refers to evidence, gives two practical suggestions and explains the result of each suggestion.

### Model 4 — Narrative: *A Rainy-Day Helper*

**Task:** Write a story about helping someone on a rainy day.

> Heavy rain was falling when Mia left the library one Saturday afternoon. She was hurrying towards the bus stop when she heard a child crying near a puddle. A little boy had dropped his homework folder, and the wind was blowing the worksheets across the wet ground.  
>
> Mia opened her umbrella over the papers and asked the boy to hold the folder. Then she picked up the worksheets carefully. A shopkeeper saw what had happened and gave her a few dry paper towels. Together, Mia and the boy dried the pages as well as they could.  
>
> Soon, the boy’s mother arrived. She thanked Mia for staying with him instead of walking away. Although Mia missed the first bus, she did not mind. On the way home, she smiled because a difficult afternoon had ended with a kind action.

**Why it works:** The opening creates a setting, the middle develops a believable problem and solution, and the ending includes a reflection. The story mixes past continuous and simple past naturally.

## Primary 6 models

### Model 5 — Proposal: *A Quiet Study Corner*

**Task:** Write a proposal to your principal suggesting how to create a quiet study corner at school.

> To: Principal Lee  
> Subject: Proposal for a Quiet Study Corner  
>
> Dear Principal Lee,  
>
> I am writing to propose a quiet study corner in the library. At present, some pupils find it difficult to complete reading or homework during recess because the playground and corridors are noisy. A small, well-organised study area would give them a calm place to work.  
>
> First, two tables could be placed near the back windows of the library. They should be separated from the borrowing desk so that pupils are not interrupted. Second, the school could provide a simple sign which says, “Quiet study in progress.” Finally, a basket of dictionaries, reading books and revision cards could be prepared by the library helpers.  
>
> This project would not require expensive equipment, but it could benefit many pupils. If the corner is successful, it may also encourage more students to use the library responsibly. I hope the school will consider this proposal.  
>
> Yours faithfully,  
> Jamie Chan

**Why it works:** The proposal identifies a genuine need, gives realistic actions, explains benefits and uses a formal, organised structure. It also includes passive forms, conditionals and persuasive language appropriately.

### Model 6 — Article: *Small Acts, Stronger Community*

**Task:** Write an article for the school magazine explaining why pupils should take part in volunteer service.

> ## Small Acts, Stronger Community
>
> Have you ever helped someone and felt happier afterwards? Volunteer service is not only about giving time. It is also a chance to understand the people around us and discover what we can do well.  
>
> For example, pupils can read stories to younger children at a community centre, help elderly visitors use simple digital devices or collect useful items for families in need. These activities may seem small, but they can make daily life easier for others. At the same time, volunteers learn to communicate, solve problems and work as a team.  
>
> Some pupils worry that they are too busy. However, even one morning each month can be meaningful. A class could begin with a simple project, such as making greeting cards for a care home or cleaning a nearby beach.  
>
> Let us take one small action this term. When pupils serve others with care, they help to build a kinder and stronger community.

**Why it works:** The article has an engaging question, clear examples, a counterpoint and a strong call to action. Paragraphing and linkers guide the reader through the argument.

## Exam-writing checklist

| Before writing | While writing | After writing |
|---|---|---|
| Underline the genre, audience and task points. | Use one paragraph for each main idea. | Check that every task point is included. |
| Decide which tense is needed. | Add a detail, example, reason or feeling to develop ideas. | Check tense consistency, verb forms, capitals and punctuation. |
| Plan an opening and an ending. | Use linkers such as *first*, *however*, *because*, *therefore* and *finally* naturally. | Replace repeated simple words with precise topic vocabulary where suitable. |
