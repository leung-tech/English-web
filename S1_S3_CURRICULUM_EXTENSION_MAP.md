# S1–S3 Curriculum Framework Extension Map

This document maps the user-provided lower-secondary English framework to newly added **original practice** modules in Secondary English Studio. The map is a teaching and revision aid. It is not an official Education Bureau year-by-year syllabus, HKEAA examination blueprint, HKDSE paper or marking scheme.

| Learning area | S1 foundation | S2 application | S3 senior-secondary bridge |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tenses** | Past continuous; present perfect continuous in school and community contexts. | Past perfect; present perfect continuous and evidence-based time relationships. | Past perfect and present perfect continuous in sentence rebuilding and formal-response contexts. |
| **Sentence structures** | Relative clauses using `who` and `where`; indirect noun-clause statement order. | `whose`, reported questions and Type 2 conditional sentences. | Relative, noun-clause, Type 2 conditional, participle opener, inversion and parallel-structure rebuilding. |
| **Voice and mood** | Passive school notices; basic reported speech; advice with `should`. | Passive reports; reported questions; possibility with `might`; formal requests. | Passive reporting, formal evidential language and careful recommendations in the existing S3 labs plus the new rebuild tasks. |
| **Verb patterns** | `enjoy + -ing` and `decide + to + verb`. | `avoid + -ing` and `plan + to + verb`. | Patterns are transferred into formal response, sentence rebuilding and writing models. |
| **Reading strategies** | Skimming, scanning, context clues, inference, reference, topic sentences and basic cohesion. | The same strategies applied to media, study routines, balanced interpretation and trial evidence. | Paired-source comparison, evidence selection, perspectives, reference, topic sentences, cohesion and balanced conclusions. |
| **Writing** | Narrative and descriptive writing with setting, sensory detail, past tenses and reflection. | Formal email with two PEEL-style points, limited evidence, concern response and polite close. | Argumentative article with introduction, two PEEL-style paragraphs, counterargument, rebuttal, evidence limits and sentence variety. |
| **Vocabulary logic** | Meaning in familiar school and community contexts. | Evidence, source, audience, register and practical recommendation language. | Twelve Lexical logic lab questions on `beneficial`, `detrimental`, `substantial`, `allocate`, `feasible`, `ambiguous`, `reinforce` and `mitigate`, together with cautious claim and connector logic. |
| **Sentence rebuilding** | Short sentence patterns are introduced through grammar contexts. | Compound/complex sentence choices are practised in grammar and writing scaffolds. | Ten clickable Sentence rebuild lab tasks on review sequence, concession, Type 2 conditional, relative clause, passive reporting, participle opening, past perfect, noun clause, inversion/parallel ideas and present perfect continuous. |

## Use sequence

Students can first complete the relevant grammar or reading-strategy module, then use the writing scaffold to apply its language in a purposeful text. S3 students can use the Lexical logic lab before Sentence rebuild lab, because precise word choice and connector logic make sentence structure easier to control. The final writing scaffold is a self-checking draft task; it does not award automatic language-quality scores.
